The COVID-19 pandemic affected almost every sector, and real estate in Greece was no exception. Offices closed, viewings stopped and buyers from abroad could not travel. Some of those changes disappeared with the lockdowns; others have become part of how property is bought in Greece today. This article first recalls what happened, as we described it in 2023, and then looks at what has stayed in 2026 and what it means for you as a buyer.

## Short answer

- **During the lockdowns** agencies closed their offices, viewings were suspended and sales slowed sharply; some sellers offered discounts.
- **Virtual viewings** became the norm for buyers abroad, and they have stayed.
- **Buying from abroad** through a power of attorney to a lawyer, signed at a Greek consulate or with an apostille, is now routine.
- **Buyers want more space:** gardens, terraces, a good internet connection and villages that live all year.
- In Halkidiki, the market recovered and prices in coastal areas have continued the steady rise that began in 2017.

## What happened during the pandemic

### The effects of lockdown

The first major consequence of COVID-19 in Greece was the extended lockdown imposed by the government to contain the virus. People were allowed to leave their homes only for essential trips, such as grocery shopping or medical appointments. Real estate agents had to close their offices and suspend scheduled viewings and meetings until further notice. This put a big dampener on sales and caused real disruption in the market.

### The impact on prices

With fewer transactions taking place, prices came under pressure. Some sellers who needed to sell offered significant discounts, and buyers who were willing to act despite the situation could find good deals. As we wrote at the time, this created a wave of buyers looking for bargains and ready to invest despite the uncertainty.

### The rise of virtual transactions

The third change was the move to virtual tours and viewings. Buyers who could not travel asked for videos and live video calls, and agents began offering them as standard. This allowed buyers from all over the world to keep looking at Greek property, and transactions continued even during lockdown periods.

## What has stayed in 2026

### Video viewings are here to stay

We still show properties by video call, and many buyers now start their search that way. A video viewing is a good way to build a shortlist without spending money on travel. Our advice: ask to see the property at two different times of day, and in the state in which it will be delivered. For a house you plan to live in, ask for a video in winter as well as in summer: a summer village in August is very different from the same village in November.

Most buyers still come for a viewing trip before they decide. We group viewings by peninsula, so you can see several properties in a day.

### Buying without being present

The pandemic made remote purchases common, and the process is now well established. In short:

1. **Greek tax number (ΑΦΜ).** Without it nothing can be signed. It is issued in one day at the tax office in Polygyros with your passport, and we accompany you or arrange it by power of attorney.
2. **Power of attorney.** You give a power of attorney to your lawyer, signed at a Greek consulate or with an apostille.
3. **Legal check.** An independent lawyer checks the title at the land registry, encumbrances, building permits and the energy certificate, before any payment.
4. **Pre-contract and deed.** Your lawyer can sign the pre-contract and the final deed before the notary on your behalf. The balance is paid by bank transfer with proof of the origin of funds.
5. **Land registry.** The deed is registered at the cadastre, and from that moment the property is yours.

The whole process typically takes four to eight weeks from the pre-contract to registration. Our [step-by-step buying guide](/en/articles/buyers/the-ultimate-guide-to-buying-property-in-halkidiki) has the details, the documents and the costs.

### More space, gardens and year-round villages

After months indoors, many people looked again at the countryside and the coast. In Halkidiki we saw more requests for houses with gardens, terraces and outdoor space, and for homes that can be used for longer periods, not only for two weeks in August. A good internet connection became a requirement for anyone planning to work remotely from the house.

That shift is still visible. Buyers now ask whether the village lives all year: is the bakery open in November, is there a pharmacy, a doctor, a school? Villages such as Nea Moudania, Nikiti, Kassandria, Ierissos, Arnaia and Polygyros have services in winter; most beach villages in Sithonia and south Kassandra close from November to April. Our guide to [Halkidiki villages](/en/articles/all-news/hidden-gems-lesser-known-villages-in-halkidiki-worth-exploring) describes the differences.

## The market after the pandemic

The bargain period did not last. In Halkidiki, prices in coastal areas have risen steadily since 2017, and the pandemic turned out to be a pause rather than a turning point. Thessaloniki airport, about an hour from the peninsulas, has added direct flights from Germany, the UK and the Balkans, and holiday letting has recovered with the return of tourism.

What has changed are the rules. In 2026 a buyer should know that:

- The transfer tax is 3.09 % of the price or the official value, whichever is higher; total costs are about 6 % on top of the price.
- New builds pay the 3.09 % transfer tax instead of 24 % VAT until 31 December 2026.
- A 15 % transfer tax for non-EU/EEA buyers of residential property has been announced for 1 July 2027. It is not law yet; see our article on the [2027 transfer tax](/en/articles/buyers/greece-property-transfer-tax-non-eu-buyers-2027).

The full figures are in our [property tax guide with calculator](/en/articles/business-news/taxes-foreigners-properties-greece#property-tax-calculator).
