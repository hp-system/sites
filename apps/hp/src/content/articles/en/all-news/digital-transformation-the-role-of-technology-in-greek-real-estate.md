In recent years the Greek property market has embraced technology in the way homes are found, checked and bought. Some changes are big: a national land registry, online tax services and viewings by video. Others are talked about more than they are used. Here is what has really changed, what still works the traditional way, and how you can use the tools to buy safely, even from abroad.

## Short answer

- **Finding a home** happens online: listings with photos, videos and maps, with full details for registered members.
- **Viewing** can start by video call; the final choice is usually made in person.
- **Checking the title** relies on the **Hellenic Cadastre**, the national land registry.
- **Taxes** are handled through **AADE**, the tax authority: Greek tax number, transfer tax and the yearly ENFIA.
- **Signing** is still traditional: the deed is signed **before a notary**, in person or through your lawyer with a power of attorney.

## 1. Online listings: the search starts on a screen

For most buyers the search begins online. Property websites bring together homes, apartments, plots and commercial buildings, with photos, floor areas and prices, and make it far easier to compare areas from home.

The weakness of online listings is quality: copied adverts, old photos and missing paperwork. That is why we photograph every listing with our own team and check the paperwork before publication. [Registered members](/en/why-register) see exact locations, all photos and videos, and can receive alerts when a price drops, free of charge. Guests see the municipality, not the exact address.

## 2. Virtual and video viewings

Virtual tours and live video calls are now a standard part of buying in Greece. They are especially useful for international buyers: you can see the layout, the condition and the view before you book a flight. We show properties by video call on request, and we group in-person viewings by peninsula so you can see several homes in one day.

Two tips: ask to see the property at two different times of day, and in the state in which it will be delivered. And if you plan to live there all year, ask for a video in winter as well.

## 3. The Hellenic Cadastre

The biggest structural change in Greek real estate is the Hellenic Cadastre (Κτηματολόγιο), the national land registry. Your lawyer uses it to check that the seller owns the property and that there are no mortgages or other encumbrances. After the deed is signed, it is registered at the cadastre, usually one to two weeks later, and from that moment the property is yours. Registration costs about 0.5 % of the value.

A reliable registry makes buying safer for foreigners. It does not remove the need for a lawyer: building permits, the energy certificate and the engineer's certificate that there is no illegal construction still have to be checked one by one.

## 4. AADE: tax online

The Independent Authority for Public Revenue (AADE) runs the Greek tax system and its online services. For a buyer, three things matter:

- **The Greek tax number (ΑΦΜ).** Nothing can be signed without it. It is issued in one day at the tax office in Polygyros with your passport; we accompany you or arrange it by power of attorney.
- **The transfer tax.** 3.09 % of the price or the official value, whichever is higher, paid by the buyer before the deed. New builds under the VAT suspension (until 31 December 2026) pay the same 3.09 % instead of 24 % VAT.
- **ENFIA.** The yearly property tax is issued automatically every spring and can be paid in up to 12 monthly instalments. An accountant files your annual return; expect about €150–250 a year.

The figures are explained in our [property tax guide with calculator](/en/articles/business-news/taxes-foreigners-properties-greece#property-tax-calculator).

## 5. Energy certificates and greener homes

Every sale requires an energy certificate, which gives the buyer a clear view of the energy class of the home. Certified green buildings get a 5–10 % discount on ENFIA. Technology inside the house matters too: heat pumps, solar panels and remote control of heating and cooling are increasingly common in new villas. See our article on [smart home technology](/en/articles/all-news/the-future-of-smart-home-technology-in-real-estate).

## 6. Data and informed decisions

Data helps buyers and investors see market trends and prices more clearly. Before you buy, we show you the sales history of the area rather than a forecast, and for investors the rental history of comparable homes where it is available. Data is a guide, not a promise: holiday-home markets depend on tourism and on the economy of the buyers' home countries. More in our article on [Halkidiki market trends](/en/articles/buyers/halkidiki-real-estate-market-trends-what-to-expect-in-the-coming-years).

## 7. Managing a property from abroad

Owners who live abroad use apps and online services to follow their property: utility bills, ENFIA, bookings for holiday lets and maintenance requests. If you plan to let the house, short-term rentals need a registration number (ΑΜΑ), and rental income is declared in your annual tax return. After the sale we can arrange property management and letting through partners if you want it.

## 8. Blockchain and smart contracts: not yet

Blockchain and smart contracts are often presented as the future of property transactions: automatic verification of ownership, transfers and payments. In Greece they do not replace the current process. A property sale is still a notarial deed, signed before a notary, with the transfer tax paid beforehand and the deed registered at the cadastre. The notary checks the identity of the parties and the legality of the transaction, which is exactly the protection a buyer needs.

## What still needs people

Technology makes the search faster and the process more transparent, but the steps that protect you still involve people: the independent lawyer who checks title and permits, the engineer, the notary, and an agent who knows the area and tells you honestly what a village is like in winter.
