## Pros and cons of selling (and buying) real estate in Greece

When it comes to buying and selling property, there are different approaches. In Greece you can work with a real estate agent, or you can try to sell or buy on your own. Both are legal, and both can work. This article sets out the honest pros and cons of each, so you can decide what fits your situation.

First, one point that is often misunderstood: **in Greece, the final contract is always signed before a notary**, and the property is registered at the land registry (cadastre). Neither an agent nor a private seller replaces the notary, and neither replaces an independent lawyer. The question is not "agent or lawyer", but who finds the buyer or the property, sets the price, organises the documents and keeps the process moving.

## Working with an agent

### The advantages

- **Knowledge of the local market.** A local agent knows what similar homes have actually sold for, not just the asking prices online. For a seller that means a realistic price from day one; for a buyer, it means knowing when a price is fair.
- **Reach.** Agents have access to listings, buyers already searching, market data and advertising that are hard for a private seller to match. A good agency also has buyers who are registered and waiting for exactly your kind of home.
- **Negotiation.** The agent negotiates on your behalf, which takes the emotion out of the conversation and usually keeps it moving.
- **Paperwork and coordination.** Selling a home in Greece needs a set of documents from the seller: title deeds and a land registry extract, the building permit, an energy certificate, an engineer's certificate that there is no illegal construction, an ENFIA certificate and tax and social security clearance. An experienced agent knows this list and coordinates the lawyer, the engineer, the notary and the tax office, which saves you a great deal of time.
- **Safety.** Viewings are accompanied, buyers are qualified before they visit, and you do not have to open your home to strangers alone.
- **For foreign owners and buyers.** If you live abroad, an agent on the spot is your eyes and ears. With a power of attorney to your lawyer, signed at a Greek consulate or with an apostille, you can complete the sale or purchase without being present.

### The disadvantages

- **Commission.** An agent charges a commission. It is agreed in advance, in writing, in the brokerage agreement you sign. Read it and ask questions before you sign.
- **Less direct control.** You hand over part of the process. Choose an agent who reports back regularly and tells you honestly what buyers say about your property.
- **Not all agents are equal.** Ask how the agency photographs and checks its listings, which languages its agents speak, and how it will reach buyers abroad.

## Selling (or buying) on your own

### The advantages

- **No commission.** Cutting out the intermediary means more of the sale price stays in your pocket, or, as a buyer, you may negotiate directly with the owner.
- **Full control.** You decide what information is shared and when, and you make every decision yourself. If you already know how property transactions work in Greece, this can suit you.

### The disadvantages

- **Pricing.** Without data on recent sales, private sellers often set the price too high, and a home that sits on the market for months becomes harder to sell. Buyers on their own risk the opposite: overpaying, or missing that the tax is due on the official value if it is higher than the price.
- **Reach.** A private advert reaches fewer buyers, and very few of those abroad. In Halkidiki, many buyers come from Thessaloniki, Bulgaria, Serbia, Germany and Romania; reaching them in their own language takes work.
- **Time.** Answering calls, arranging viewings, chasing documents and coordinating the professionals is close to a part-time job.
- **Risk.** Paperwork problems such as unpermitted extensions or missing certificates often surface late. Without someone who has seen them many times, they can delay or break a sale.

## What does not change either way

Whether you work with an agent or not, Greek law sets the same steps: a Greek tax number for the buyer, the legal check of title and permits, a pre-contract (usually with a deposit of about 10 %), transfer tax of 3.09 % paid by the buyer before the deed, the notary deed and registration at the cadastre. Costs for the buyer come to about 6 % on top of the price. For sellers, one more point: the 15 % capital gains tax for private individuals is suspended until 31 December 2026, so a private seller currently pays no tax on the profit.

## So which is right for you?

It depends on your situation and your goals:

- **Go solo** if you know the Greek market and procedures well, you have the time, you are in Greece, and your documents are complete.
- **Work with an agent** if you are new to buying or selling in Greece, you live abroad, you want the widest reach to buyers, or you simply want the process to run smoothly according to Greek law, with less time and stress on your side.

Whichever path you choose, use an independent lawyer, sort out the documents before you set a price or pay a deposit, and get the full cost in writing.
