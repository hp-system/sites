If you're looking for a stunning destination in Greece, Halkidiki should be at the top of your list. I recently had the opportunity to explore this beautiful region on a one-day trip, and I was blown away by its natural beauty and rich culture. Below is the day as I lived it, followed by the practical notes you need to repeat it, and what to look at if, like many of our buyers, you fall for the place and start thinking about a house.

## Why a day trip works for Kassandra

Halkidiki has three peninsulas, and you cannot see all of them in a day. Kassandra, the first one, is the easiest: it is about 70 km from Thessaloniki airport, the road is good all the way, and its villages sit close together. It is also the peninsula with the sandy beaches, the resorts and the busiest summer life. Sithonia, greener and quieter, and the Mount Athos side with Ierissos and Ouranoupoli each deserve a day of their own.

The route is simple. From Thessaloniki you drive south-east past Nea Moudania, the year-round town of the area, and cross the canal at Nea Potidea onto Kassandra. From there the villages of the peninsula are spread along both coasts.

## Morning: the charming village of Afytos

My day started in the charming village of Afytos (also written Afitos), on the [Kassandra peninsula](/en/property-for-sale/kassandra), about 80 km from the airport. As I walked through the narrow streets, I felt as though I had stepped back in time. The traditional architecture and [stone houses](/en/properties) spoke of a rich history and culture, and I couldn't wait to explore more.

Afitos sits on a cliff above the Toroneos gulf. The old core is stone-built, with cobbled lanes, small galleries and craft workshops, and a square where the tavernas look straight down to the sea. Below the cliff there is a long sandy beach, reached by paths and steps from the village. Go early: the light on the gulf is at its best in the morning, the lanes are quiet, and you can walk the whole village in an hour without hurrying.

What to look for while you walk:

- the stone houses of the old core, many renovated with modern interiors behind traditional facades;
- the views from the edge of the square across the gulf towards Sithonia;
- the paths down to the beach, if you want a swim before lunch.

## Lunch: traditional Greek cuisine

After a delicious lunch at a local taverna, where I indulged in traditional Greek cuisine, I was ready for the afternoon. In Halkidiki a taverna lunch is not a quick stop. Order a few plates to share: grilled fish or meat, a Greek salad, local cheese, olives and bread, and take your time. Halkidiki is known for its green olives, its honey and its wine, and the tavernas of Afitos and the neighbouring villages serve them all.

## Afternoon: Sani Beach, a jewel of Kassandra

After lunch I headed to Sani Beach on the Kassandra peninsula. The golden sand and turquoise waters of the Aegean Sea were breathtaking, and I couldn't resist taking a dip. The warm sun and salty sea air were the perfect combination for a relaxing afternoon.

Sani is one of the best-known names in Halkidiki: a resort area with long sandy beaches, a marina and a protected wetland behind the coast. It is a short drive from Afitos across the peninsula. In summer the beach is busy but long enough to find space; in late spring and early autumn it is calm and the sea is still warm.

## Evening: back to Afytos

As the sun began to set, I headed back to Afytos to explore some more. The evening scene was bustling with locals and tourists alike, all enjoying the warm hospitality of the village. I stumbled upon a local bakery and couldn't resist trying some baklava, a delicious sweet pastry that is a staple of Greek cuisine.

Evening is when Afitos is at its liveliest. The square fills up, the galleries stay open, and the view over the gulf turns gold and then dark blue. If you are driving back to Thessaloniki the same night, leave after dinner rather than at sunset: the summer traffic back towards the city is lighter later in the evening.

## Practical notes for your own day trip

- **Car.** You need one. Public buses connect Thessaloniki with the Kassandra villages, but a car lets you combine Afitos and Sani in one day.
- **Season.** May, June and September are the best months for a day trip: warm sea, fewer people. In July and August start early.
- **Winter.** Afitos is one of the few Kassandra villages with life in winter. Many beach villages further south close from November to April.
- **What to bring.** Swimwear, walking shoes for the cobbled lanes, cash for small bakeries and kiosks.
- **More time?** Add a second day for Sithonia: Nikiti old town, Neos Marmaras and the hill village of Parthenonas. Our guide to [lesser-known villages](/en/articles/all-news/hidden-gems-lesser-known-villages-in-halkidiki-worth-exploring) describes them.
