If you're looking to sell your house, chances are you're trying to make it as attractive as possible. One of the most important aspects of presenting a home for sale is making sure its decor looks welcoming and inviting. Here are the home decorating and staging tips we give sellers in Halkidiki, from the first clean to the documents that keep a sale on track, so you make a great impression on potential buyers.

## Cleanliness is key

The first thing potential buyers will notice when they walk into your home is how clean it looks and feels. Make sure your place is spotless from top to bottom and don't forget about areas like the windows, doors, baseboards, shelves, and light switches! Take the time to clear away any clutter so that your space looks spacious and inviting.

Decluttering is half the work. Put away personal photos, collections and the things that pile up on kitchen counters and bathroom shelves. Buyers need to imagine their own life in the house, and they cannot do it surrounded by yours. Empty a few cupboards too: buyers open them, and half-empty storage looks like plenty of storage.

## Air out a closed-up summer house

Many homes in Halkidiki are holiday homes that stay closed for months. A house shut since September smells of it in spring, and buyers notice at the door. Before viewings:

- open all windows and shutters for a few hours, on more than one day;
- run the air conditioning or the heating so the rooms feel at a normal temperature;
- check for damp patches, especially in ground-floor rooms and behind furniture, and treat them before you paint;
- turn on the water and flush the taps, so nothing runs brown during a viewing;
- clear the garden, the terrace and the pool area of leaves and winter debris.

If you live abroad, ask a neighbour, a cleaner or your agent to do this before each round of viewings.

## A fresh coat of paint

Nothing freshens up a space quite like a new coat of paint. Choose neutral colours that have mass appeal, nothing too bold or unique. A fresh coat of paint can make all the difference in how appealing a space appears to buyers. In a seaside house, look also at the outside: salt and sun fade shutters, railings and outdoor woodwork quickly, and a repaint there is one of the cheapest ways to make a house look cared for.

## Let in natural light

People love natural light! Open up curtains and blinds throughout the day to let in natural sunlight and brighten up dark corners with lamps. Making sure there is plenty of light around will help create an airy atmosphere and attract buyers who appreciate comfort and brightness. Replace dead bulbs, use the same warm colour of light in every room, and clean the glass of doors and windows inside and out. In Halkidiki the view is often what sells the house: make sure nothing blocks it.

## Update your furniture

Update furniture pieces if necessary so that they look fresh and modern; no one wants to buy furniture from another decade. Invest in some new pillows or throws for couches or chairs if needed: these small touches can go a long way towards making your house look more inviting.

Less is more. Take out one or two pieces from each room so buyers can move around easily and see the real size of the space. Decide early whether you sell furnished or empty. Many buyers of holiday homes like a furnished house they can use or let straight away; others want to bring their own. Either is fine, as long as it is clear, and what stays with the house is written into the pre-contract.

## Show the outdoor life

In Halkidiki buyers are buying a summer life as much as a house. The terrace, the garden, the barbecue and the pool are often what they remember after a day of viewings.

- Set the outdoor table with chairs and a parasol, even out of season.
- Cut the grass, trim hedges and water the plants a few days before the viewing.
- Clean and fill the pool, or cover it neatly in winter.
- Fix outdoor lights, so an evening viewing still shows the garden.
- Make the parking space and the entrance easy to find.

## Summer and winter viewings

Holiday homes sell all year, but they look different in each season. In summer show the shade, the breeze and the walk to the beach. In winter show that the house is dry, warm and easy to heat: turn the heating on an hour before buyers arrive and have last summer's photos ready on a tablet. Many of our buyers want to know how a house feels in November before they commit, so a well-prepared winter viewing is an advantage, not a handicap.

A large share of buyers in Halkidiki shortlist from abroad, so the first viewing usually happens on a screen. That is why we photograph every listing with our own team and do not copy adverts. You help by having the house ready on the day of the shoot: clean, decluttered, all lights on, outdoor furniture out, pool clean. A short video walk-through, from the entrance through every room to the garden and the view, lets a buyer abroad decide whether to fly in. ## Have your documents ready

Decor makes buyers want the house; documents let them buy it. In Greece the buyer's lawyer checks the title and permits before any deposit is paid, and missing papers are the most common reason a sale slows down. Before you list, gather:

- title deeds and a land registry (cadastre) extract;
- the building permit;
- the energy certificate;
- the engineer's certificate of no illegal construction;
- the ENFIA certificate and tax and social security clearance.

The seller pays for the engineer's certificates and the energy certificate. If something is missing or a past addition was never declared, find out now, not when a buyer is waiting. The best staging cannot sell an overpriced house. ## Ready to sell?

Selling your home involves more than just sticking a sign on the lawn: it takes effort, time, energy, money and creativity to pull off successfully. Follow these simple tips to make sure your house looks its best for potential buyers. With these steps in mind, you'll be able to show potential buyers how great their future life could be if they decide to purchase this property.
