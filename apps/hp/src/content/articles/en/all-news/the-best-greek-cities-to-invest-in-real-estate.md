Greece has long been a favourite destination for tourists, and it is also one of the most interesting places in Europe to buy property. The climate, the views and the way of life draw buyers from all over the world. But Greece is not one market: a flat in Athens, a seaside house in Halkidiki and a villa on Mykonos are three different investments, with different prices, seasons and rules. Here is how the main markets compare in 2026, and how to choose between them.

## Short answer

- **Athens** for year-round city demand and the deepest market, at the €800,000 Golden Visa threshold.
- **Thessaloniki** for a large, active city market in the north, also at €800,000.
- **Halkidiki** for a holiday home or a seasonal let an hour from Thessaloniki airport, with lower entry prices and the €400,000 Golden Visa threshold.
- **The popular islands** for high-end summer lets, at higher prices, with a shorter season and the €800,000 threshold.

The buying costs are the same everywhere: about 6 % on top of the price.

## Athens

The capital is home to more than 3 million inhabitants and is the largest city in Greece. Its prices have risen in recent years, helped by its popularity with tourists, which makes it attractive to investors looking for demand all year. Athens offers easy access to public transport and numerous entertainment options, and its rental demand does not stop at the end of summer.

**Think about:** Attica is in the €800,000 Golden Visa zone, so a residence permit through property costs twice as much as in Halkidiki. In a city flat, the condition of the building and the shared costs matter as much as the flat itself.

## Thessaloniki

The second-largest city in Greece, on the northern coast, is known for its cultural heritage, its food and its nightlife. It has one of the most active property markets in the country, with demand all year, making it a good place for investors looking for a busy market with potential upside.

**Think about:** Thessaloniki is also in the €800,000 Golden Visa zone. Its real advantage for many of our buyers is what lies next to it: Halkidiki starts less than an hour away, and many Thessaloniki families own a second home there, which keeps the Halkidiki market busy outside the summer too.

## Halkidiki

If you are looking for something calmer than Athens or Thessaloniki, consider Halkidiki: a peninsula of blue and green on the northern mainland, with 550 km of coast, Blue Flag beaches, and plenty of outdoor activities such as hiking and fishing. Property prices are reasonable compared with other popular parts of Greece, which makes it a good choice if you want an affordable investment by the sea.

- **[Kassandra](/en/properties/kassandra):** sandy beaches, resorts and the strongest rental demand.
- **[Sithonia](/en/properties/sithonia):** pine forests, coves, villas with sea views; quieter.
- **[Aristotelis](/en/properties/aristotelis):** towards Mount Athos, the lowest prices per square metre and large plots.
- **[Nea Propontida](/en/properties/nea-propontida):** 40–60 km from Thessaloniki, schools, hospital and shops all year; the best area for a permanent home or a year-round let.

Seasonal lets of well-located houses near the beach typically bring a gross return of about 4–6 % of the purchase price a year; year-round lets near Nea Moudania or Polygyros bring less but with no empty months. Buyers come from Greece, Bulgaria, Serbia, Germany and Romania, which gives a wide resale market. The full picture is in [houses for sale in Halkidiki: prices and areas](/en/articles/all-news/homes-for-sale-in-halkidiki-greece) and in [why invest in Greek real estate](/en/articles/buyers/investing-in-real-estate).

**Think about:** many beach villages close from November to April. If you want to live there or let all year, choose a town that lives in winter.

## The islands: Mykonos, Santorini and the rest

The famous islands attract an international luxury market. Prices for comparable homes are often two to three times those of Halkidiki, the season is shorter (June to September), and access depends on seasonal flights or ferries. Mykonos, Santorini and islands with more than 3,100 residents are in the €800,000 Golden Visa zone.

**Think about:** island property can be rewarding in high-end letting, but it needs a bigger budget and a manager on the island for most of the year.

## What is the same everywhere

- **Buying costs:** transfer tax 3.09 % of the price or the official value, whichever is higher; notary and lawyer about 1 % each plus VAT; land registry about 0.5 %. About 6 % in total. Our [cost calculator](/en/articles/business-news/taxes-foreigners-properties-greece#property-tax-calculator) gives the exact figure.
- **Yearly tax:** ENFIA, charged per square metre by the zone value of the location, so it is higher in expensive areas.
- **Letting:** short-term lets need a registration number (ΑΜΑ); rental income is taxed from 15 % after a 5 % deduction.
- **Selling:** no capital gains tax for private individuals until 31 December 2026.
- **Non-EU buyers:** a 15 % transfer tax on homes has been announced for 1 July 2027, not yet law. See [the 2027 transfer tax for non-EU buyers](/en/articles/buyers/greece-property-transfer-tax-non-eu-buyers-2027).
- **The process:** a Greek tax number, an independent lawyer, a pre-contract and a notary deed; four to eight weeks. See the [step-by-step buying guide](/en/articles/buyers/the-ultimate-guide-to-buying-property-in-halkidiki).

## How to choose

Start from the goal, not the place. If you want a year-round rental in a city, Athens or Thessaloniki. If you want a holiday home that also earns in summer, near an airport, at a lower price, Halkidiki. If you are after luxury on a famous island and the budget allows, a famous island. And if residency matters, remember that the same Golden Visa costs €400,000 in Halkidiki and €800,000 in the big cities and the most famous islands.
