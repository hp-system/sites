We've all heard about the rise of smart home technology and its impact on the way we live, work and play. But did you know that this technology is also changing the way people buy, own and sell homes? For a buyer in Halkidiki the question is practical: most homes here are holiday homes, and many of their owners live in another country for most of the year. Smart technology is how you keep an eye on a house you are not in. Here is what it does, what it is worth, and what to check before you buy a house that has it.

## Why it matters more for a holiday home

A house by the sea in Halkidiki is often closed from October to May. In that time a lot can happen that nobody sees: a pipe leaks, the power trips and the freezer thaws, damp builds up in a ground-floor room, a shutter is left open in a storm. With a few connected devices you know about it the same day, from your phone, wherever you are. That is the real value of a smart home for our buyers: not gadgets, but fewer surprises when you open the house in spring.

## Smart home automation for buyers

For homebuyers, one of the most useful aspects of smart home technology is automation. Automation allows you to control a wide variety of devices, from lighting to temperature to security systems, with your voice or a mobile device. This makes it easier than ever to stay connected to your home, no matter where you are. You can adjust settings while away on holiday or on business. Automation can also save you money by optimising energy use and reducing costs related to heating, cooling and lighting.

### Heating and cooling from a distance

The most used feature is the simplest: turning on the air conditioning or the heating before you arrive. In winter a closed house needs to be kept above a minimum temperature to stay dry; in summer you want to walk into a cool house after the drive from the airport. Smart thermostats and connected air-conditioning units do both, and they show you the temperature and humidity inside the house at any moment.

### Leak, power and humidity sensors

Small, inexpensive sensors under sinks, next to the water heater and near the washing machine send an alert when they detect water; some can close the main valve automatically. A power monitor tells you when the electricity goes off, and a humidity sensor warns you before damp becomes mould. For a house that stays closed for months, these are the first devices we suggest.

## Smart security systems

Another benefit of smart home technology is security. Many smart security systems include motion sensors, video cameras and two-way audio that let you keep an eye on your property at all times. These systems are often integrated with lights or door locks for added convenience and protection. A smart lock with temporary codes also solves a very practical problem: you can let in the cleaner, the gardener, the pool technician or a guest without leaving keys with a neighbour.

## Smart app integration

Many smart products come with their own apps that let you manage your devices from anywhere in the world. You can monitor energy use in real time, or set schedules so that shutters close at night and lights switch on in the evening, which makes an empty house look lived in. Choose systems that can be grouped in one app; a house with six different apps is a house nobody wants to manage.

## Pools, gardens and outdoor systems

In Halkidiki the outdoor systems often matter more than the indoor ones. Pool pumps, filters, heating and chlorination can be scheduled and monitored remotely, and irrigation can water the garden on a timer that adapts to the season. Outdoor lighting on a schedule keeps the garden safe and the house visible.

## A local example: Villa Natalia

Our own construction company built [Villa Natalia](/en/articles/constructors-news/villla-natalia) in Sithonia as a smart house. Temperature, humidity, underfloor heating and cooling, air conditioning, lighting, locking and unlocking, pool temperature and air quality can all be controlled from a phone or tablet, and each room has its own wall-mounted panel. Geothermal pumps and solar panels on the roof reduce the energy needs of the villa, and both pools are cleaned automatically, with natural chlorination by electrolysis and ozone for the large one. Not every home needs all of this, but it shows what is possible in a Halkidiki house today.

## Energy, the energy certificate and taxes

Smart systems save money mainly through energy: heating only the rooms you use, cooling the house only before you arrive, using solar power when it is available. Every home sold in Greece needs an energy certificate, which the seller provides, and efficient systems help a better rating.

On taxes, be precise. A smart home does not by itself reduce your annual property tax. The ENFIA discounts in 2026 are 5–10 % for certified green buildings and 20 % for homes insured against earthquake, fire and flood for the full year (10 % above €500,000 of value). The details are in our [property tax guide](/en/articles/business-news/taxes-foreigners-properties-greece).

## What to check before you buy a smart home

- **The list of systems** and who installed them; ask for manuals and the installer's contact.
- **Accounts and apps.** Make sure the seller's accounts are transferred or reset to you at the deed, and that nothing depends on a subscription in the seller's name.
- **Internet.** Everything depends on a stable connection; ask what the house uses and test it during the viewing.
- **Permits.** Solar panels, heat pumps and outdoor installations should be declared; your engineer and lawyer check this as part of the [legal check before the pre-contract](/en/articles/buyers/the-ultimate-guide-to-buying-property-in-halkidiki).
- **Local service.** Ask who maintains the systems in the area. A clever system without a local technician becomes a problem.

## Smart homes and resale

For a buyer abroad, a house that can be watched and managed remotely is easier to own and easier to let. Systems that are simple, well documented and maintained add to a property's appeal; complicated, half-working ones do the opposite.
