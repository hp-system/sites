Every property in Greece has two values: the price you agree with the seller, and an official value set by the state, known as the objective value (αντικειμενική αξία). For a buyer the official value matters more than most people expect: it sets the floor for the transfer tax and it is the basis of the yearly property tax, ENFIA. This page explains how it works today and why the state has been adjusting it, with our original 2020 article kept below as background.

## Short answer

- The **objective value** is the state's official value of a property, based on the zone price of its location and coefficients for the property itself.
- The **transfer tax** (3.09 %) is paid on the **higher** of the agreed price and the objective value.
- **ENFIA**, the yearly property tax, is calculated per square metre from the zone price: **€2.50 to €16.25 per m²**, multiplied by coefficients.
- The municipal property fee (**TAP**) is 0.025–0.035 % of the official value, charged through the electricity bill.
- Your lawyer should give you **both values** before you sign anything.

## How the objective value is calculated

Greece is divided into zones, and each zone has an official price per square metre. The objective value of a specific property starts from that zone price and is adjusted with coefficients: the size of the property, its age, the floor of an apartment, whether it has a facade on the street, and other characteristics. The result is an administrative value. It does not follow the market day by day; it changes when the state revises the zones.

That is why the official value and the real price can be far apart. In a sought-after seaside village the market price is often higher than the official value. In some inland areas, or for properties in poor condition, the official value can be close to the price or even above it.

## Why it matters when you buy

### Transfer tax

The transfer tax of 3.09 % is calculated on the price or the official value, **whichever is higher**. If the official value is lower than the price, you pay on the price. If it is higher, you pay on the official value, even though you paid less.

A simple illustration: if the agreed price were €200,000 and the official value €220,000, the 3.09 % would be calculated on €220,000. This is one of the most common surprises for foreign buyers, which is why our [step-by-step buying guide](/en/articles/buyers/the-ultimate-guide-to-buying-property-in-halkidiki) lists "ignoring the official value" among the mistakes to avoid.

### New builds

New buildings sold under the VAT suspension (until 31 December 2026) also pay the 3.09 % transfer tax, on the same basis. More in our article on the [VAT suspension on new builds](/en/articles/buyers/the-new-regulation-relief).

## Why it matters every year

### ENFIA

ENFIA (ΕΝΦΙΑ, the unified property tax) is issued every spring and can be paid in up to 12 monthly instalments. The main tax is €2.50 to €16.25 per square metre depending on the zone price of the location, multiplied by coefficients for age, floor and facade. In Halkidiki most holiday homes fall between €2.5 and €10 per m²: a 100 m² house in a coastal village typically pays €400–700 a year.

If your total Greek property value exceeds about €400,000–500,000, a surcharge of 0.1–1 % is added on the excess. Discounts exist: 20 % for homes insured against earthquake, fire and flood for the whole year, and others listed in our [property tax guide](/en/articles/business-news/taxes-foreigners-properties-greece).

### The municipal fee

TAP, the municipal property fee, is 0.025–0.035 % of the official value and is charged through the electricity bill: usually a few euros a month.

## The 2020 adjustment: what we wrote at the time

Our original article, published in December 2020, described a reform then under way. It is kept here as the picture of that moment:

- The modification of the objective prices was aimed at **closing the gap between selling prices and objective prices** across Greece, and the government was also working on a new method of estimating them, expected at the beginning of 2020.
- About **7,000 areas** were to be brought into the new system, with an expected revenue of about **€400–500 million**.
- The example used at the time: a villa of 550 m² in Mykonos, for sale at €3.5 million, was paying just **€1,010 a year** in ownership tax. Abnormalities like this were expected to change with the new values.
- The extra revenue was meant to **reduce other taxes**, such as those on inheritance and on transfers between parents and children.
- The ownership tax was to be modified: higher in some cases, but significantly lower for low incomes.
- Objective prices were expected to **rise** in specific areas of Athens and in famous destinations such as Mykonos and Santorini.

The direction set then still holds: the official value is meant to move closer to real prices over time. Because it can change when the zones are revised, check the current figure for each property rather than relying on an old valuation.

## What to do as a buyer

1. **Ask for both values early.** Your lawyer or the notary calculates the official value at the start of the legal check, before any deposit.
2. **Budget on the higher figure.** Use the higher of price and official value for the transfer tax, then add notary, lawyer and land registry: about 6 % in total. Our [calculator](/en/articles/business-news/taxes-foreigners-properties-greece#property-tax-calculator) gives the figure for any price.
3. **Estimate the ENFIA** before you choose between two similar homes: a larger plot or a better zone can mean a higher yearly bill.
4. **Keep the insurance discount in mind**: 20 % off ENFIA for a home insured for the full year.
