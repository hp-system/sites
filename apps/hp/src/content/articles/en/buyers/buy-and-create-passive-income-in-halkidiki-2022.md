One of the first questions we ask a buyer is whether the purchase is **an investment or for pleasure.** Over the last years the answer we hear most often is: **both**.

Since platforms such as Airbnb appeared, owners of holiday homes let them for short or longer periods during the season, while still spending part of their own holidays there. Many of our buyers list their Halkidiki property on several platforms as soon as the deed is signed. Here is how that combination works in practice, what it can earn, what it costs, and the rules that apply in 2026.

## Short answer

A holiday home in Halkidiki that you also let can realistically pay for its own running costs and your holiday, and in a good location bring a gross return of about 4–6 % of the purchase price a year. You need a registration number (ΑΜΑ) for short-term letting, rental income is taxed from 15 % after a 5 % deduction, and every week you keep for yourself in July and August is a week you do not let.

## Two ways owners split the year

- **Let in high season, stay in the quiet months.** The house is let in July and August, when demand and prices are highest, and the owners come in May, June, September or October, when Halkidiki is calmer and the sea is still warm.
- **Holiday in July and August, let the rest.** Families with school children often keep the high season for themselves and let the house in the shoulder months.

Both groups share the same idea: to get something back from the money they spent on the property.

## Be honest about the goal

Letting in Halkidiki as a stand-alone business can be profitable. But if you want to spend a couple of months, or even a couple of weeks, in the house yourself, you may break up a long booking in the best part of the season. In that case profit is not your number one goal. The usual target of owners who combine business and pleasure is simpler: to collect from the rent enough to pay for their own holidays and the annual upkeep of the house.

And no matter how beautiful Halkidiki is, one summer you may want to travel somewhere else. That year, let the house for the whole season and enjoy the higher income.

## What it can earn

Seasonal lets from May to October of well-located houses near the beach typically bring a gross return of about 4–6 % of the purchase price a year, with the owner using the house in May and October. Year-round lets near Nea Moudania or Polygyros bring a lower rate but no empty months, because these towns live all year.

Location decides most of the result. [Kassandra](/en/property-for-sale/kassandra) has the sandy beaches, the resorts and the strongest rental demand. [Sithonia](/en/property-for-sale/sithonia) is greener and quieter, with guests who come for coves and pine forests. [Aristotelis](/en/property-for-sale/aristotelis) has the lowest prices per square metre. [Nea Propontida](/en/property-for-sale/nea-propontida) is 40–60 km from Thessaloniki and lets all year. We do not promise figures in advance; for a specific property we show you the rental history of comparable homes where it is available.

## What it costs to own

- **Buying:** about 6 % on top of the price: 3.09 % transfer tax, notary and lawyer about 1 % each plus VAT, land registry about 0.5 %. The [cost calculator](/en/articles/business-news/taxes-foreigners-properties-greece#property-tax-calculator) gives the exact figure.
- **ENFIA every year:** a 100 m² house in a coastal village is typically €400–700, and 20 % less if it is insured against earthquake, fire and flood for the full year.
- **Municipal fee (TAP):** usually a few euros a month, charged through the electricity bill.
- **Swimming pool:** a pool adds the luxury living tax, about €3,000 a year for a 100 m² outdoor pool.
- **Accountant:** about €150–250 a year for your annual Greek tax return.
- **Running the let:** cleaning, linen, platform commissions, repairs and a manager if you use one. These depend on the property; ask us for the figures of a specific house.

## The letting rules in 2026

- **Registration number (ΑΜΑ).** Every property let short-term through Airbnb, Booking or similar platforms needs one.
- **Climate resilience fee.** Paid by the guest for every night.
- **No VAT for small owners.** Owners of up to two short-term properties pay no VAT on the rent.
- **Rental income tax.** After a flat 5 % expense deduction: 15 % up to €12,000, 25 % from €12,001 to €24,000, 35 % from €24,001 to €35,000 and 45 % above. Example: €15,000 of rent leaves €14,250 taxable and €2,363 of tax.
- **Selling later.** Private individuals pay no capital gains tax until 31 December 2026.

All the details are in our [property tax guide for foreigners](/en/articles/business-news/taxes-foreigners-properties-greece).

## Choosing a property that lets well

- **Walking distance to a beach.** Guests pay for it, and it matters more than size.
- **The right type.** [Apartments](/en/property-for-sale/apartments) are the most affordable way in, easy to let, with low running costs. Houses in small complexes cost less to maintain and are easier to let. [Maisonettes](/en/property-for-sale/maisonettes) suit families who want a garden.
- **A village that matches your plan.** For summer lets, a beach village is enough. For year-round lets, choose a town that lives all year, such as Nea Moudania, Nikiti or Polygyros.
- **Practical details.** Parking, air conditioning, outdoor space and a good internet connection are what guests check first.
