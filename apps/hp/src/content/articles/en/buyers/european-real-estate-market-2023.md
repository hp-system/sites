## Are you considering buying your first home?

Buying your first home is a big step anywhere in Europe, and buying it in another country adds a language, a tax system and a legal procedure you do not know yet. Prices have risen in many European markets, and interest rates have moved a lot in recent years, so careful planning matters more than ever. If you are a first-time buyer looking at Greece, and Halkidiki in particular, this guide covers the three things that decide whether the purchase goes well: a realistic budget, the right financing and the right people around you.

## 1. Build a budget that includes every cost

The most important step is to understand your finances before you look at a single property. Sit down and work out how much you have saved, how much you are willing to borrow, if anything, and what monthly costs you can carry afterwards.

The most common mistake is to budget for the price only. **In Greece, buying costs about 6 % on top of the price:**

| Item | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Transfer tax | 3.09 % of the price or the official value, whichever is higher |
| Notary | about 1 % plus 24 % VAT |
| Lawyer (recommended) | about 1 % plus 24 % VAT |
| Land registry | about 0.5 % |

For a €250,000 house that comes to about €15,175. New builds sold under the VAT suspension, which runs until 31 December 2026, pay the same 3.09 % instead of 24 % VAT. Our free [cost calculator](/en/articles/business-news/taxes-foreigners-properties-greece#property-tax-calculator) gives the exact figure for any price.

Then add a reserve for furniture, small works and the first year of running costs. Every year you will pay:

- **ENFIA**, the property tax: in Halkidiki most holiday homes fall between €2.5 and €10 per m²; a 100 m² house in a coastal village is typically €400–700 a year, 20 % less if the home is insured against natural disasters;
- **a small municipal fee** through the electricity bill;
- **an accountant** for your Greek tax return, about €150–250 a year;
- electricity, water, insurance and, in a complex, the shared maintenance costs.

If you are not an EU or EEA citizen, check one more item: a 15 % transfer tax for non-EU buyers of homes has been announced for 1 July 2027. It is not law yet; our article on the [2027 transfer tax](/en/articles/buyers/greece-property-transfer-tax-non-eu-buyers-2027) explains who it may affect.

## 2. Decide how you will pay

Once you understand your finances, look at how to fund the purchase.

- **Cash.** Most of our foreign buyers pay cash, often from savings or the sale of another property. It keeps the process simple and gives you a stronger position in negotiation.
- **A loan in your home country.** Many buyers borrow at home, for example against their main residence, where the bank already knows them.
- **A Greek mortgage.** Greek banks lend to non-residents on a case-by-case basis, usually up to 50–60 % of the value, and ask for full income documentation. Start early if you go this way.

Wherever you borrow, shop around: different lenders offer different rates and conditions, and some offer incentives that make them more attractive than others. Consider carefully whether an adjustable-rate mortgage suits you. It may start with a lower rate, but after the initial period the rate can rise significantly, which can strain your finances if you have not planned for it.

Whatever you choose, the payment at the deed is made by bank transfer, and you must show **proof of the origin of the funds** (bank statements, a sale contract, salary). Prepare these documents in advance.

## 3. Choose where to buy

On a first purchase, location matters more than anything else: you can renovate a house, but you cannot move it. In Halkidiki the choice is between four quite different markets:

- **[Kassandra](/en/property-for-sale/kassandra)**: sandy beaches, resorts, the strongest rental demand;
- **[Sithonia](/en/property-for-sale/sithonia)**: greener and quieter, pine forests and coves;
- **[Aristotelis](/en/property-for-sale/aristotelis)**: the lowest prices per square metre, large plots;
- **[Nea Propontida](/en/property-for-sale/nea-propontida)**: 40–60 km from Thessaloniki, services all year, best for a permanent home.

As a guide to price levels, apartments start around €100,000 and houses with a garden near the sea around €200,000. Decide also whether this is a holiday home, a permanent home or partly a rental: a permanent home needs a village that lives all year, and many beach villages close from November to April. Our overview of [houses for sale in Halkidiki](/en/articles/all-news/homes-for-sale-in-halkidiki-greece) compares the areas in detail.

## 4. Choose an experienced local agent

An experienced agent is invaluable on a first purchase abroad. A good local agent knows which areas buyers are moving to, what homes have actually sold for, and which properties have clean paperwork. They can tell you about the services nearby, schools if you have children, and the things that affect a home's value now and in the future.

Look for an agent who:

- knows the local market and the Greek rules and taxes;
- photographs and checks the properties personally;
- speaks your language and answers quickly;
- works with independent lawyers and notaries rather than replacing them.

Alongside the agent, use an **independent lawyer**. It is not compulsory in Greece, but the lawyer checks the title, encumbrances and permits before you pay any deposit, and that is the step where problems surface.

## 5. Know the steps before you start

Buying in Greece follows a fixed sequence: Greek tax number (issued in one day), the lawyer's check, a pre-contract with a deposit of about 10 %, the transfer tax, the notary deed and registration at the land registry. It usually takes four to eight weeks. Our [step-by-step buying guide](/en/articles/buyers/the-ultimate-guide-to-buying-property-in-halkidiki) explains each step, the documents you need and the mistakes we see most often.

## Conclusion

Buying a home is one of the most important financial decisions you will make. With careful planning it does not have to be overwhelming: build a budget that includes every cost, settle your financing early, choose the location before the house, and work with an experienced local agent and an independent lawyer. Then take your time to explore the options before deciding.
