Halkidiki has long been the summer destination of northern Greece. Over the last decade it has also become one of the most active holiday-home markets in the country, driven by its natural beauty, better access and a broader mix of buyers. Below are the trends we see on the ground as a local agency, what they mean for you as a buyer, and the changes in the rules that will shape the next few years.

## Short answer

- **Demand is broad and steady.** Greeks, Bulgarians, Serbians, Germans and Romanians buy here, which keeps the resale market wide.
- **Prices in coastal areas have risen steadily since 2017**, as Thessaloniki airport added direct flights from Germany, the UK and the Balkans.
- **The top end is growing:** villas with sea views and pools in Sani, Nikiti, Vourvourou, Afitos or Possidi.
- **Holiday letting is a big part of the story**, with a season from May to October.
- **Rules are moving:** the VAT suspension on new builds and the capital gains suspension run until 31 December 2026; a 15 % transfer tax for non-EU buyers has been announced for July 2027.

## 1. Demand: more buyers, more reasons to buy

Tourism in Greece has grown strongly in recent years and Halkidiki has benefited directly. Many buyers first came here on holiday. Today they buy for three reasons, often at once: a summer house for the family, an asset that earns rent in the season, and a place to live later, for example in retirement.

Access matters. Thessaloniki airport is about one hour by car from the peninsulas, all year, and the city itself sends a steady stream of weekend visitors. That gives Halkidiki a longer season than many islands and a local market that does not depend only on foreigners. [Nea Propontida](/en/properties/nea-propontida), the belt closest to Thessaloniki, lives twelve months a year and attracts permanent residents as well as holiday buyers.

## 2. Prices: up steadily, but not the same everywhere

Prices in coastal Halkidiki have risen steadily since 2017. The rise has not been uniform: seafront land in places such as Vourvourou is among the most expensive in Halkidiki, while [Aristotelis](/en/properties/aristotelis) on the Athos side still has the lowest prices per square metre. Today apartments start from about €100,000, houses with a garden near the sea from about €200,000, plots from about €100,000, and villas from about €600,000. All prices on our site are live, so you see the market as it is.

We do not promise appreciation. Before you buy, we show you the sales history of the area, which is a better guide than any forecast.

## 3. The rise of luxury villas

Halkidiki is seeing more high-end homes: architect-designed villas with panoramic views of the Aegean, private pools, large plots and, in a few cases, direct access to the beach. [Sithonia](/en/properties/sithonia) offers villas with sea views among the pine forests; [Kassandra](/en/properties/kassandra) has the resort areas such as Sani and Afitos. Many of these properties are above the €400,000 Golden Visa threshold, which brings in buyers from outside the EU. [See villas for sale](/en/property-for-sale/villas).

## 4. Holiday letting and short-term rentals

The popularity of Halkidiki with visitors keeps demand for short-term rentals strong in the season. Many owners let the house in July and August and use it themselves in May, June or September; others do the opposite. Seasonal letting of a well-located house near the beach typically brings a gross return of about 4–6 % of the purchase price a year; year-round lets near Nea Moudania or Polygyros bring less but with no empty months.

Letting has become more regulated: short-term rentals need a registration number (ΑΜΑ), guests pay a climate resilience fee per night, and rental income is taxed from 15 % after a 5 % expense deduction. The details are in our [property tax guide](/en/articles/business-news/taxes-foreigners-properties-greece).

## 5. More types of property, and more new builds

Beside the classic apartments and maisonettes, the market now includes boutique hotels, small resorts, renovated stone houses in villages such as Arnaia and Parthenonas, and developments of new homes. New builds have been helped by the suspension of the 24 % VAT, which runs until 31 December 2026: buyers pay the 3.09 % transfer tax instead. Off-plan purchases can cost less than finished homes and let you choose finishes, but the deed date and the developer's tax choice must be checked by your lawyer. More in our article on the [VAT suspension](/en/articles/buyers/the-new-regulation-relief).

## 6. Greener homes

Buyers increasingly ask about insulation, solar panels, heat pumps and energy class. Every sale requires an energy certificate, and certified green buildings get a 5–10 % discount on ENFIA, the yearly property tax. Developers in Halkidiki are responding with better-insulated homes and more use of local materials. For a holiday home that stays empty part of the year, lower running costs are a real advantage.

## 7. Smart homes

Remote control of heating, cooling, lighting, locks and pool equipment is now common in new villas, and it makes sense for owners who live abroad. You can check the house from your phone and prepare it before you arrive. We look at what is worth paying for in our article on [smart home technology](/en/articles/all-news/the-future-of-smart-home-technology-in-real-estate).

## 8. A place to diversify

With uncertainty in other markets, many buyers see a property in Halkidiki as a way to spread their savings: an asset priced in euro, which can earn rent and be used by the family. It is not risk-free. Holiday-home markets depend on tourism and on the economy of the countries the buyers come from, which is why we recommend buying a home you would also enjoy using.

## What could slow or change the market

- **The announced 15 % transfer tax for non-EU buyers.** On 6 September 2026 the government announced a 15 % transfer tax on residential property for buyers from outside the EU and EEA, from 1 July 2027. It is not law yet. If you are a British, Serbian, North Macedonian, Swiss or American buyer, read our article on the [2027 transfer tax](/en/articles/buyers/greece-property-transfer-tax-non-eu-buyers-2027).
- **The end of two suspensions.** The VAT suspension on new builds and the 15 % capital gains tax suspension for private sellers both end on 31 December 2026 unless they are extended.
- **The Golden Visa thresholds.** Halkidiki is in the €400,000 zone, against €800,000 in Attica, Thessaloniki, Mykonos, Santorini and the large islands. Any change to these zones would affect the top end of the market.
