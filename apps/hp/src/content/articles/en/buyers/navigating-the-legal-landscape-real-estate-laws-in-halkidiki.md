Halkidiki is part of Greece, and property there follows Greek law: the same rules, the same notaries, the same land registry as in the rest of the country. The good news for foreign buyers is that the legal path is clear and well trodden. Below is what the law requires at each stage, what your lawyer checks, and where buyers most often run into trouble. This is general information, not legal advice: for your own purchase, an independent lawyer looks at the specific property.

## Who can buy

**Anyone.** EU citizens buy without any restriction, and non-EU citizens (British, Serbian, Swiss, American and others) also buy freely, because Halkidiki is not a restricted border zone: no special permit is needed. Foreign buyers follow exactly the same procedure as Greeks.

The one thing every buyer needs is a **Greek tax number (ΑΦΜ)**. It is issued in one day at the tax office in Polygyros with your passport; we accompany you, or your lawyer arranges it with a power of attorney. Without it nothing can be signed. If you cannot be in Greece for the signing, a power of attorney to your lawyer, signed at a Greek consulate or with an apostille, lets the purchase go ahead without you.

## The legal steps of a purchase

1. **Due diligence by your lawyer.** Before any payment, an independent lawyer checks the title at the land registry, any mortgages or other encumbrances, the building permit and the energy certificate. This is the step where problems surface, which is why it comes first.
2. **Pre-contract and deposit.** A private or notarial pre-contract fixes the price, the delivery date and what is included (furniture, appliances, fixtures). A deposit of about 10 % is usual; the property then comes off the market.
3. **Transfer tax.** The buyer pays 3.09 % before the deed, calculated on the price or on the official ("objective") value, whichever is higher. New builds sold under the VAT suspension, which runs until 31 December 2026, pay the same 3.09 % instead of 24 % VAT.
4. **The notary deed.** The final contract is signed before a notary, who ensures the legality of the transaction. The balance is paid by bank transfer with proof of the origin of the funds.
5. **Registration.** The deed is registered at the Hellenic Cadastre (land registry). From that moment the property is legally yours. Electricity, water and the annual property tax are then transferred to your name.

Typical timing is four to eight weeks from pre-contract to registration. Costs come to about 6 % on top of the price: the full breakdown is in our [step-by-step buying guide](/en/articles/buyers/the-ultimate-guide-to-buying-property-in-halkidiki).

## The documents the seller must provide

Your lawyer collects these from the seller before the deed:

- title deeds and an extract from the land registry;
- the building permit;
- the energy performance certificate;
- an engineer's certificate that there is no illegal construction on the property;
- an ENFIA certificate and tax and social security clearance.

The engineer's certificate deserves attention. In Greece, extensions, closed verandas or extra rooms built without a permit are a common issue. If they were legalised, the documents show it; if not, the problem must be solved before the sale, not after.

## Land use and building rules

Halkidiki protects its coast and forests, and what you may build depends on where the land is:

- **Plots inside a settlement plan** and **fields outside it** have very different building rights: how much you can build, how high, and on how large a plot. Never assume a field can take a house because the neighbour has one. Your engineer checks the limits before you sign; we show them before the viewing. Our page on [plots for sale in Halkidiki](/en/articles/all-news/halkidiki-plots-for-sale) explains what to look for.
- **Protected traditional settlements**, such as Arnaia, have renovation rules for facades and materials. Your engineer checks what is allowed before you plan any work.
- **Mount Athos itself** is a monastic community where property cannot be bought; the villages around it, such as Ouranoupoli and Ierissos, follow the normal rules.

## Your obligations as an owner

Owning property in Greece comes with a few recurring duties:

- **ENFIA**, the unified property tax, is issued every spring and can be paid in up to 12 monthly instalments. In Halkidiki most holiday homes fall between €2.5 and €10 per m² a year; a 20 % discount applies if the home is insured against earthquake, fire and flood for the full year.
- **A small municipal fee** is charged through the electricity bill.
- **An annual tax return (E1)**, usually filed by an accountant for about €150–250 a year.
- **If you rent out the home:** short-term rentals need a registration number (ΑΜΑ), and rental income is taxed after a flat 5 % deduction, at 15 % up to €12,000, 25 % up to €24,000, 35 % up to €35,000 and 45 % above.

All the rates are in our [property tax guide for foreigners](/en/articles/business-news/taxes-foreigners-properties-greece).

## Changes on the horizon

On 6 September 2026 the government announced a 15 % transfer tax for non-EU/EEA buyers of residential property, planned from 1 July 2027. **It is an announcement, not a law**: today everyone pays 3.09 %. Land and commercial property are excluded from the announced measure, and the final law will define exemptions and transitional rules. Our article on the [2027 transfer tax for non-EU buyers](/en/articles/buyers/greece-property-transfer-tax-non-eu-buyers-2027) follows the details.

For private sellers, the 15 % capital gains tax is suspended until 31 December 2026.

## If something goes wrong

Most disputes are avoided by doing the checks in the right order. When they do happen, they are usually resolved by negotiation between the lawyers, with the pre-contract as the reference: that is why its terms (price, delivery date, what is included, what happens if a document is missing) matter. Only if that fails does a case go to the Greek courts.

## Mistakes we see most often

- Paying a deposit before the lawyer's report.
- Ignoring the official value: tax is due on the higher of price and official value.
- Buying land without checking what can be built on it.
- Leaving unpermitted extensions to "sort out later".
- Assuming a company structure is a simple tax shortcut; decide ownership with your accountant before the pre-contract.
