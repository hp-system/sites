## Have you ever dreamed of owning a vacation home that overlooks the Mediterranean Sea?

If so, you have probably considered buying a property in Greece. Owning a holiday home here has real benefits, but it also comes with costs and responsibilities that are easy to underestimate from a sun lounger in August. Here are the pros and cons as we see them with our buyers in Halkidiki, with the real 2026 figures, so you can decide whether it is right for you.

## Short answer

A vacation home in Greece is right for you if you will use it regularly, accept the yearly costs and either let it or keep it in good hands while you are away. Expect about 6 % on top of the price when you buy, a yearly ENFIA property tax (typically €400–700 for a 100 m² coastal house in Halkidiki), and income tax from 15 % if you let it. The purchase itself is straightforward: four to eight weeks with a lawyer and a notary.

## The pros

### The place itself

The main benefit is the country: beaches, pine forests running down to the sea, ancient sites and villages where life follows the seasons. In Halkidiki you have three peninsulas, each with its own character, an hour from Thessaloniki airport.

### Prices compared with the rest of Europe

Greece remains an affordable destination compared with many parts of Europe. In Halkidiki, apartments near the beach start from about €100,000 and houses with a garden from about €200,000, well below the popular islands, where comparable homes often cost two to three times more. Lower costs for your guests also mean you can charge competitive rates when you let.

### Guests who want to come

Tourism is a pillar of the Greek economy. A well-located house near a beach is easy to let from May to October, and the weekends from Thessaloniki and the Balkans extend the season. Seasonal lets of well-located beach houses typically bring a gross return of about 4–6 % of the purchase price a year. How owners combine their own holidays with letting is explained in [buy and earn from your Halkidiki home](/en/articles/buyers/buy-and-create-passive-income-in-halkidiki-2022).

### A straightforward purchase

Buying in Greece is not as difficult as many people think. You need a Greek tax number (issued in one day), an independent lawyer for the legal check, a pre-contract with a deposit, and the final deed before a notary. EU and non-EU citizens can buy, and Halkidiki is not a restricted border zone, so no permit is needed. The full sequence is in our [step-by-step buying guide](/en/articles/buyers/the-ultimate-guide-to-buying-property-in-halkidiki).

### Tax rules that still favour private owners

Private individuals pay no capital gains tax when they sell until 31 December 2026, and new builds sold under the VAT suspension until the same date pay 3.09 % transfer tax instead of 24 % VAT.

## The cons

### Taxes and running costs

These costs are not exorbitant, but they add up if you do not plan for them:

- **When you buy:** transfer tax 3.09 % of the price or the official value, whichever is higher, plus notary and lawyer (about 1 % each plus VAT) and land registry (about 0.5 %). Example: €21,245 for a €350,000 resale home.
- **Every year:** ENFIA, the property tax, charged per square metre by zone. In Halkidiki most holiday homes fall between €2.5 and €10 per m²: a 100 m² coastal house is typically €400–700 a year, 20 % less if insured for the full year against earthquake, fire and flood.
- **Municipal fee (TAP):** usually a few euros a month through the electricity bill.
- **A swimming pool** adds the luxury living tax, about €3,000 a year for a 100 m² outdoor pool, and a deemed minimum income that matters only if your real Greek income is lower.
- **An accountant** for your annual Greek tax return: about €150–250 a year.
- **Upkeep:** garden, pool, repairs after winter, insurance.

The details for each item are in our [property tax guide for foreigners](/en/articles/business-news/taxes-foreigners-properties-greece), with a calculator for your own numbers.

### Letting means rules and tax

If you let the house, short-term lets through Airbnb, Booking and similar platforms need a registration number (ΑΜΑ). The rental income is taxed separately from your other income after a flat 5 % deduction: 15 % up to €12,000, 25 % up to €24,000, 35 % up to €35,000 and 45 % above. The guest pays the climate resilience fee per night, and owners of up to two short-term properties pay no VAT.

### Distance

A house you visit a few times a year needs someone on the spot: to check it after storms, open it in spring, welcome guests and handle repairs. Plan this before you buy. Property management is available through our partners.

### Summer villages close in winter

Many beach villages in Sithonia and south Kassandra close from November to April. That is fine for a summer house, less so if you want to spend Christmas there. Towns such as Nea Moudania, Nikiti, Kassandria, Ierissos and Polygyros live all year. See [villages worth knowing before you buy](/en/articles/all-news/hidden-gems-lesser-known-villages-in-halkidiki-worth-exploring).

### Paperwork must be checked

Title, encumbrances, building permits and the energy certificate must be checked by your lawyer before you pay any deposit. Problems surface at this stage, and that is exactly why it comes first.

### A possible change for non-EU buyers

On 6 September 2026 the government announced a 15 % transfer tax for non-EU/EEA buyers of residential property from 1 July 2027. It is not law yet. If you are a British, Serbian, Swiss or American buyer, read our article on the [2027 transfer tax for non-EU buyers](/en/articles/buyers/greece-property-transfer-tax-non-eu-buyers-2027).

## So, is it right for you?

Ask yourself three questions. Will you use the house often enough to enjoy it? Can you carry the yearly costs, or will letting cover them? Do you have someone you trust on the spot? If the answers are yes, a holiday home in Greece can be one of the best decisions you make. If not, wait or rent first.

Do your homework, understand the costs before you commit, and you will avoid surprises down the line.
