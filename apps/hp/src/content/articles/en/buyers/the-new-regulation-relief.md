A new home in Greece would normally carry 24 % VAT. Since 2020 the state has suspended that VAT for new buildings, and the suspension has been extended: under Law 5246/2025 it runs until **31 December 2026**. While it applies, you pay the ordinary **3.09 % transfer tax** instead, exactly as for a resale home. This page explains who qualifies, what it saves you, what to check, and why the measure was introduced in the first place.

## Short answer

- New buildings with a building permit issued **from 1 January 2006** onwards, sold as new by the developer, can be sold **without 24 % VAT** until 31 December 2026.
- The buyer pays the **3.09 % transfer tax** on the price or the official value, whichever is higher.
- The developer **may opt out** of the suspension, so the contract must say which regime applies.
- All other costs stay the same: notary, lawyer and land registry bring the total to about **6 % on top of the price**.

## What the suspension means in euros

The difference is large. Take a new house priced at €300,000. With 24 % VAT the tax alone would be €72,000. Under the suspension the buyer pays the 3.09 % transfer tax: about €9,270. For the full picture of a purchase (tax, notary, lawyer, land registry) use the [cost calculator in our property tax guide](/en/articles/business-news/taxes-foreigners-properties-greece#property-tax-calculator); for a €350,000 home the total on top of the price is €21,245, about 6.07 %.

This is why we always ask a developer, before a viewing, two questions: is the building covered by the suspension, and will the deed be signed within the period of the law?

## Who qualifies

### The building

The suspension concerns new buildings whose building permit was issued from 1 January 2006 onwards. It applies to the first sale by the developer, not to a home that has already been sold and is now resold: a resale always pays the 3.09 % transfer tax anyway.

### The developer's choice

The law lets the developer opt out of the suspension. In practice most developers in Halkidiki sell under the suspension, because it makes their homes cheaper for the buyer, but you should never assume it. The pre-contract should state the tax regime in writing.

### The timing

The suspension is tied to the period set by the law, currently until 31 December 2026. If you are buying off-plan or the building will be completed later, the date of the final deed matters. Ask your lawyer which regime will apply on the date you expect to sign, and do not pay a deposit before you have that answer.

## What to check before you buy a new build

1. **The building permit and its date.** Your lawyer checks it at the start of the legal review, together with the title at the land registry and any encumbrances.
2. **The tax regime in the contract.** VAT suspended (3.09 % transfer tax) or VAT applied (24 %). One line in the pre-contract avoids a very expensive misunderstanding.
3. **The official value.** The transfer tax is calculated on the higher of the price and the official value; your lawyer tells you both before you sign.
4. **The energy certificate** and the engineer's certificate that there is no illegal construction.
5. **The delivery date** and what happens if it slips beyond the end of the suspension period.

The full sequence of a purchase, with timing and documents, is in our [step-by-step buying guide](/en/articles/buyers/the-ultimate-guide-to-buying-property-in-halkidiki).

## Why the measure was introduced (2020)

When the suspension was first announced in 2020, it was seen as a major relief for the Greek property market. Our article at the time listed the reasons it was considered a game changer. They are kept here as the view of that moment, not as current figures:

- **New buyers for unsold new homes.** All properties built after 2006 and still unsold became more attractive. The rough estimate at the time was that there were more than 100,000 such properties across the country.
- **Foreign buyers and investors.** It was expected to attract new foreign buyers, including those interested in the Golden Visa programme.
- **Cash flow for construction companies.** Developers would sell faster, improve their cash flow and invest in new projects.
- **Jobs.** More than 160 professions connected to construction were expected to benefit directly, helping reduce unemployment, which had been a serious issue in Greece for years.

At the time we hoped that the measure would mark the start of a new era of construction and financial growth. Six years later, new developments are a visible part of the market in Halkidiki, from small complexes of maisonettes to villas with pools.

## How it fits with the other 2026 rules

- **Golden Visa.** In Halkidiki a single property of at least 120 m² bought for €400,000 or more gives a five-year renewable residence permit. A new villa under the VAT suspension can meet the threshold without the 24 % VAT on top.
- **Selling later.** The 15 % capital gains tax for private individuals is suspended until 31 December 2026.
- **Non-EU buyers.** A 15 % transfer tax for non-EU/EEA buyers of residential property was announced for 1 July 2027. It is an announcement, not law. Details in our article on the [2027 transfer tax for non-EU buyers](/en/articles/buyers/greece-property-transfer-tax-non-eu-buyers-2027).

Rules change, and both the VAT suspension and the capital gains suspension end on 31 December 2026 unless they are extended again. We update this page when the law changes.
