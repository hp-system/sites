Halkidiki, in northern Greece, is one of the most popular holiday destinations in Europe. Its clear water, beaches and scenery make it the place where many people dream of owning a home. That demand also makes the market confusing for a first-time buyer, especially from abroad. Below are the questions our agents hear most often, with straight answers and the 2026 figures.

## 1. How is the real estate market in Halkidiki doing?

Demand is strong. Tourism has grown, and since the pandemic more people want a home on the coast or in the countryside. Good homes near the sea are limited, and prices in coastal Halkidiki have risen steadily since 2017, as Thessaloniki airport added direct flights from Germany, the UK and the Balkans.

What we will not do is promise that prices will keep rising. Before you buy, we show you the sales history of the area so you can judge for yourself. If you are buying partly as an investment, our article on [investing in Greek real estate](/en/articles/buyers/investing-in-real-estate) has the honest numbers: seasonal lets near the beach typically return about 4–6 % gross a year.

## 2. Can foreigners buy property in Halkidiki?

Yes. **EU citizens and non-EU citizens (British, Serbian, Swiss, American and others) buy freely**, because Halkidiki is not a restricted border zone and no special permit is needed. Foreign buyers follow the same procedure as Greek buyers. What you do need is a Greek tax number (ΑΦΜ), issued in one day at the tax office in Polygyros with your passport; we accompany you, or your lawyer arranges it with a power of attorney.

## 3. Do I need to hire a real estate agent?

It is not compulsory, but it is highly recommended if you do not know the local market, the rules and the taxes. A local agent shows you properties that match your criteria, groups viewings so you see more in less time, helps you negotiate, and coordinates the lawyer, the notary, the engineer and the tax office. A good agent also introduces you to local contractors and helps with inspections after you buy. Our article on [going solo or working with an agent](/en/articles/all-news/going-solo-or-working-with-an-agent) weighs both options.

## 4. What types of property can I buy?

The range is wide: traditional stone houses in villages, apartments and studios near the beach, maisonettes in small complexes, detached houses with a garden, modern villas with a pool, plots of land and tourism properties. Take your time and research the region before choosing; location, access to services, distance to the beach and your budget matter more than anything else. Our guide to [houses for sale in Halkidiki](/en/articles/all-news/homes-for-sale-in-halkidiki-greece) compares the types and areas, and you can browse [all properties](/en/properties).

## 5. Which area should I choose?

- **[Kassandra](/en/properties/kassandra)**: the closest peninsula to the airport, sandy beaches, resorts and the strongest rental demand.
- **[Sithonia](/en/properties/sithonia)**: greener and quieter, pine forests down to the sea, villas and plots with sea views.
- **[Aristotelis](/en/properties/aristotelis)**: towards Mount Athos, the lowest prices per square metre and large plots.
- **[Nea Propontida](/en/properties/nea-propontida)**: 40–60 km from Thessaloniki, schools, a hospital and shops all year; the best choice for a permanent home.

## 6. What are the legal requirements?

Buying a home in Greece is a notarial process. In short: Greek tax number; an independent lawyer checks the title at the land registry, encumbrances, building permits and the energy certificate; a pre-contract with a deposit of about 10 %; transfer tax paid before the deed; the final deed signed before a notary; registration at the land registry. A lawyer is not compulsory, but we always recommend one, and the legal check comes before any payment. More detail is in our guide to [real estate law in Halkidiki](/en/articles/buyers/navigating-the-legal-landscape-real-estate-laws-in-halkidiki).

## 7. How long does it take?

Usually **four to eight weeks** from the pre-contract to registration at the land registry, provided the seller's paperwork is complete. Viewings can be arranged within days, in person or by video. The full sequence is in our [step-by-step buying guide](/en/articles/buyers/the-ultimate-guide-to-buying-property-in-halkidiki).

## 8. How much should I budget?

Your budget depends on your goals, your finances and what you plan to do with the home. As a guide to price levels at the time of writing:

| Type | From about |
| --- | --- |
| Apartments | €100,000 |
| Maisonettes | €200,000 |
| Houses with a garden near the sea | €200,000 |
| Plots and land | €100,000 |
| Seafront villas | €600,000 |

Then add **about 6 %** for taxes and fees: transfer tax 3.09 %, notary and lawyer about 1 % each plus VAT, land registry about 0.5 %. Keep a reserve for furniture, small works, insurance and utilities. Every year you pay the ENFIA property tax (a 100 m² house in a coastal village is typically €400–700) and an accountant for your annual return (about €150–250). Our [calculator](/en/articles/business-news/taxes-foreigners-properties-greece#property-tax-calculator) gives the exact figure for any price.

If you are not an EU or EEA citizen, note that a 15 % transfer tax on homes has been announced for 1 July 2027. It is not law yet; the details are in our article on the [2027 transfer tax for non-EU buyers](/en/articles/buyers/greece-property-transfer-tax-non-eu-buyers-2027).

## 9. Can I get a mortgage?

Greek banks lend to non-residents case by case, usually up to 50–60 % of the value, with full income documentation. Most of our foreign buyers pay cash or borrow in their home country.

## 10. Does a property give me residency?

A single property of at least 120 m² bought for €400,000 or more in Halkidiki qualifies for the Golden Visa, a five-year renewable residence permit for you and your family. Halkidiki is outside the €800,000 zones of Attica, Thessaloniki, Mykonos, Santorini and the large islands.

## 11. What is the weather like?

Halkidiki has a Mediterranean climate: hot, dry summers from May to September and mild, rainy winters between November and February. The season for beach life and letting runs from May to October.

## 12. What services are available all year?

The larger towns have supermarkets, banks, post offices, restaurants, bars and shops all year, and the area has health centres and hospitals. Nea Moudania, Nea Kallikratia, Kassandria, Nikiti, Neos Marmaras, Ierissos, Arnaia and Polygyros have schools, shops and doctors in winter; most beach villages in Sithonia and south Kassandra close from November to April. The beaches are among the best in Greece, with plenty of water sports in summer.
