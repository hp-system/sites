- q: How can I make an appointment to visit the property I'm interested in?
  a: After you have found the property you are interested in on our website, leave a request in any way convenient for you, so that our experienced agents can arrange a visit to the property at a convenient day and time for you.
- q: What is the process of buying a property?
  a: 'After receiving all the necessary documents, your lawyer will conduct a search of the property and legally check it for the last 20 years. It will ensure market legitimacy and check for mortgages, court decisions, etc. You will also need a civil engineer to inspect the property. Also, make an advance to secure the contract. The warranty is refundable in the event of legal problems, but will be withheld if the buyer cancels the purchase at their own risk.

    FMA Property Transfer Tax is payable prior to final signing of the contract. Then you sign the contract and the seller gets the money.'
- q: What is the final price of the certain property?
  a: When it comes to the negotiation part, there are some steps that precede the final established property price. Firstly, it is necessary to find several property options that would meet the client's criteria. Then it is necessary for the client to come to Greece and visit selected properties in order to check and compare his expectations VS the real picture. Based on these comparisons and factors, the client would choose the certain property and create his own estimation and give an offer to the owner. At this point, the owner is willing to negotiate due to the fact that he deals with real and serious potential buyers.
- q: What are the supporting documents of real estate purchase and sale?
  a: The Seller of the property is responsible for the collection of all the supporting documents. Supporting documents on the part of the Buyer are required only in case he requests the exemption of the transfer tax for the purchase of a first home.
- q: What documents do I need to buy a property in Greece?
  a: All you need is your passport and a Tax Authorisation Number which can be issued in just one day at any Tax authority office in Greece.
- q: What are the costs - expenses of the buyer?
  a: 'In addition to the agreed price for the purchase, there are some additional costs that burden the buyer:

    • Transfer tax of 3.09 % on resale homes, on the price or the official value, whichever is higher. New builds carry 24 % VAT instead, suspended until 31 December 2026 (the 3.09 % transfer tax applies meanwhile).

    • Land registry fees of about 0.5 % of the price.

    • Lawyer''s fee

    • Notary fee

    • Real Estate Agent Fee'
- q: What are the extra costs that a Buyer needs to pay in order to buy a property in Greece?
  a: 'The extra costs are a group of services that are essential to finalize a purchase contract. There are some which are obligatory and others which are optional.

    Obligatory

    • Legal Control

    • Lawyer Fees

    • Notary Fees

    • Agent Fees

    • Purchase Tax

    • Registration Certificate

    Optional

    • Translations

    • Interpretations

    • Termination Clause Contract

    • Termination Clause Registration Certificate'
- q: What is the amount I have to pay for the extra costs?
  a: 'Under the 2026 rules a buyer pays about 6 % on top of the price: 3.09 % transfer tax, about 1 % notary and 1 % lawyer plus VAT, and about 0.5 % land-registry fees. Example: about €21,245 for a €350,000 resale home, before the agency commission. Our property cost calculator in the article on property tax in Greece for foreigners works out the figures for your own case.'
- q: What is the down payment I have to pay?
  a: There is no fixed amount or percentage. Each market is different, depending on the situation or desire of the seller and buyer. As a company we suggest the down payment should not be small or large as long as there are no problems. An intermediate solution of about 10% of the market is ideal and safe between buyer and seller.
- q: What is the amount of a deposit that I need to pay and I am secured about it?
  a: Before giving any kind of deposit you need to give a stop-saling guarantee. This is usually an amount of 2% to 5% of the selling price. This amount is being given to the Agent and not to the Seller as to stop advertising the property online. After this payment it's time for the legal control. In case that the legal control is not crystal clear then the Agent is returning a full refund of the deposit to you. However, if the results of the legal control are ok then you have to wire the amount of 10% minus the stop sales guarantee that you have already paid directly to the bank account of the seller. In between the stop sales guarantee and the remaining of the deposit a private agreement is being signed in between you, the Seller and the Agent as to keep you on the safe side. Please notice that the percentages of the stop sales guarantee as well as the deposit could be negotiated.

- q: How long will it take to complete the purchase?
  a: The completion time of a contract depends on many factors. The procedures have been simplified with the use of technology, but there is still bureaucracy. So, considering that the property you have chosen does not have a legal problem and what remains is to follow the standard procedure, then the time period ranges from 4 to 6 weeks. However, there are cases where the process may take a little longer due to some issues that may arise such as the legalization of additional square meters, loan repayment, missing documents, etc.

After this period of time, we can arrange an appointment at the notary office to complete the purchase. - q: Is it possible to buy in installments?
  a: Generally, if the seller wishes and agrees, real estate can be bought and sold with the specific procedure. Usually this is done only in large purchase amounts. In smaller amounts the owner probably does not agree.
- q: What options do I have when it comes to the way of payment?
  a: Many potential buyers are buying their new house in Greece by getting a loan from banks of their country. You always have the possibility though, through good negotiation, to come on an agreement with the seller as to pay part of the amount in instalments.

- q: Do I need a Greek bank account to buy a property in Greece?
  a: In order to buy a house in Greece, you DO NOT need a Greek bank account. You can send the purchase amount from your bank account everywhere in the world directly to the bank account of the Seller. A Greek bank account might be needed on a later stage for paying your utilities but yet again you can make these payments from abroad as well.

- q: What should I do after buying a property in Greece?
  a: The first and most important thing is to sit down and enjoy your property. Later, you will need to register your new property with Real Estate and manage your bill payments. Nothing more than that. An accountant could run all of these processes for you.
- q: What is legal control and why it is so important?
  a: The legal control is the first and most important action that needs to take place after you choose a house to buy. A lawyer visits the hypothec house and checks all the past deeds as to make sure that that there are no mortgages, loans, debts or any other legal loose ends. At the end of the lawyer's research if the results are clear you will receive a certificate by the legal firm. In the case that the legal control shows that there are legal issues then the lawyer is informing you about them along with the proposals for solving them. It is up to you then if you wish to move forward with the purchase or not.
- q: What is fair value and how is it calculated?
  a: The objective value of a property is the price that the tax authorities give to each property throughout Greece. There are many factors that determine this price such as the exact location of the property, its size, year of construction etc.
